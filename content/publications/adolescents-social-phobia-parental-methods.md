## Abstract

This field study examined the relationship between parental treatment methods and social phobia prevalence among adolescents in Erbil city center. The research surveyed 402 high‑school students using validated psychological assessment scales to evaluate social anxiety levels and parental disciplinary approaches.

## Methodology

The study employed standardized scales validated through Cronbach's alpha for reliability assessment. A total of 402 high‑school students from various schools in Erbil city center participated in the survey. Data collection focused on social phobia symptoms and parental treatment methods.

## Key Findings

The research revealed several important findings:

- **Social Phobia Prevalence**: Teenagers in Erbil demonstrated very low levels of social phobia
- **Parenting Styles**: Parents predominantly used democratic parenting approaches
- **Demographic Factors**: No significant differences were found by gender, grade level, or school type
- **Parent-Child Relationship**: Democratic parenting was associated with lower social anxiety levels

## Implications

These findings have implications for:

- Mental health intervention strategies for adolescents
- Parent education programs focusing on effective parenting methods
- Understanding cultural factors in adolescent mental health

## Conclusion

The study highlights the positive association between democratic parenting and low social phobia levels in adolescents, providing valuable insights for mental health practitioners and families in the Kurdistan region.
