## Abstract

This study evaluated the quality and safety of imported composts available in Erbil city, assessing both their fertilizing potential and heavy metal content. The research examined composts from various countries to determine their suitability for agricultural use in the Kurdistan region.

## Methodology

The study employed a completely randomized design to evaluate composts imported from different countries. Quality assessments included analysis of fertilizer indices and clean indices, measuring nutrient content and heavy metal contamination levels.

## Key Findings

The research revealed important quality indicators:

- **Fertilizer Index**: Values ranged from 4.13 to 4.47, indicating good nutrient content
- **Clean Index**: Values ranged from 3.20 to 3.93, suggesting medium heavy‑metal content
- **Overall Quality**: The composts demonstrated good quality suitable for agricultural land use
- **Heavy Metal Assessment**: Medium levels of heavy metals were detected, within acceptable agricultural limits

## Implications

These findings have practical applications for:

- Agricultural producers selecting compost materials
- Ensuring soil health and crop safety
- Environmental management and sustainable agriculture practices

## Conclusion

The study confirms that imported composts in Erbil meet quality standards for agricultural use, providing valuable information for farmers and agricultural professionals in the region while highlighting the importance of regular quality monitoring.
