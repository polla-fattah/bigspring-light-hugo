## Abstract

This occupational health study assessed air quality impacts and heavy metal exposure among gas station employees using biomonitoring techniques. The research utilized scalp hair and dust analysis to measure occupational exposure levels and assess health risks.

## Methodology

The study employed biomonitoring methods including scalp hair analysis and workplace dust sampling to assess heavy metal exposure. Gas station employees were evaluated for exposure levels, and environmental samples were analyzed for heavy metal contamination.

## Key Findings

The research revealed important findings:

- **Exposure Assessment**: Quantification of heavy metal exposure levels in gas station workers
- **Biomonitoring Data**: Heavy metal concentrations measured in scalp hair and workplace dust
- **Occupational Risks**: Identification of health risks associated with workplace exposure
- **Environmental Impact**: Assessment of air quality impacts from gas station operations

## Implications

These findings have important applications for:

- Occupational health and safety regulations
- Worker protection programs and exposure monitoring
- Environmental health standards in the petroleum retail industry

## Conclusion

The study demonstrates measurable heavy metal exposure in gas station employees, highlighting the importance of workplace safety measures and regular biomonitoring to protect worker health and ensure air quality standards.
