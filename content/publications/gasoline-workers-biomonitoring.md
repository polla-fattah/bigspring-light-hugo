## Abstract

This cross-sectional biomonitoring study investigated the health effects of occupational heavy metal exposure among gasoline station workers in Erbil city. The research assessed hematological parameters and heavy metal concentrations in exposed workers compared to controls.

## Methodology

The study examined 75 gasoline station workers and 25 control subjects in Erbil. Blood samples were analyzed for complete blood counts, while heavy metal concentrations were measured in scalp hair and workplace dust samples. Statistical analysis was performed to identify correlations between exposure and health effects.

## Key Findings

The research revealed significant health impacts:

- **Hematological Changes**: Exposed workers showed decreased hemoglobin and hematocrit levels
- **Blood Cell Alterations**: Increased red and white blood cell counts were observed
- **Lymphocyte Changes**: Higher lymphocyte percentages in exposed workers
- **Exposure Biomarkers**: Heavy metal levels in scalp hair and dust correlated with hematological alterations
- **Occupational Risk**: Workers demonstrated measurable health effects from occupational exposure

## Implications

These findings have important implications for:

- Occupational health and safety regulations
- Worker protection programs and medical monitoring
- Environmental health standards in the petroleum industry

## Conclusion

The study demonstrates significant hematological alterations in gasoline station workers exposed to heavy metals, emphasizing the need for improved occupational health protection measures and regular biomonitoring to safeguard worker health.
