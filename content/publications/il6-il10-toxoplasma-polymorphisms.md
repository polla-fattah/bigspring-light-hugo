## Abstract

This study investigated the relationship between cytokine gene polymorphisms and susceptibility to *Toxoplasma gondii* infection. The research assessed IL‑6‑174G/C and IL‑10‑1082A/G polymorphisms in 40 women with confirmed toxoplasmosis and 30 healthy controls to determine genetic factors influencing infection risk.

## Methodology

Genetic analysis was performed on blood samples from 40 women diagnosed with toxoplasmosis and 30 healthy control subjects. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and genotyping techniques were employed to identify specific polymorphisms in the IL‑6 and IL‑10 cytokine genes.

## Key Findings

The study revealed significant associations between cytokine polymorphisms and infection susceptibility:

- **IL‑6‑174 CC Genotype**: Tripled the risk of *Toxoplasma gondii* infection compared to other genotypes
- **IL‑10‑1082 Mutant Heterozygote**: Demonstrated a protective effect against infection
- **Genetic Susceptibility**: Specific cytokine gene variants were associated with differential infection risk
- **Clinical Implications**: Genetic screening may help identify individuals at higher risk of toxoplasmosis

## Implications

These findings contribute to understanding the genetic basis of susceptibility to *Toxoplasma gondii* infection and may inform:

- Risk assessment strategies for high-risk populations
- Development of targeted prevention approaches
- Understanding individual differences in immune response to parasitic infections

## Conclusion

The study highlights the important role of cytokine gene polymorphisms in determining susceptibility to toxoplasmosis, providing valuable insights for both clinical practice and public health interventions.
