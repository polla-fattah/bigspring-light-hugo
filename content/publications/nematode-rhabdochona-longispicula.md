## Abstract

This study investigated the monthly occurrence and maturation time of the spirurine nematode *Rhabdochona longispicula* in *Cyprinion macrostomus* fish populations along the Greater Zab River. The research collected fish samples monthly from three strategic points during 2022 to determine infection prevalence and analyze host factors affecting parasitism.

## Methodology

Monthly sampling was conducted from three distinct locations along the Greater Zab River throughout 2022. Fish specimens were examined for nematode infections, with detailed analysis of parasite maturation stages and prevalence rates.

## Key Findings

The study revealed several important findings:

- **Seasonal Patterns**: Peak infection prevalence occurred during May–June, reaching up to 42.85%
- **Host Sex**: No significant correlation was found between host sex and infection prevalence
- **Age Factor**: Older fish demonstrated higher prevalence rates, suggesting age-related susceptibility
- **Maturation Dynamics**: The research provided insights into the maturation timeline of *Rhabdochona longispicula* within its host

## Implications

These findings contribute to understanding the epidemiology of fish parasites in the Greater Zab River ecosystem and may inform fisheries management and disease control strategies for native fish populations.

## Conclusion

The temporal and host factor analysis provides valuable information for managing parasitic infections in freshwater fish populations, with particular relevance for local fisheries and aquatic ecosystem health.
