## Abstract

This research investigated the use of green-synthesized nanoscale zero‑valent iron (nZVI) nanoparticles for remediating heavy metal-contaminated soils near oil refinery sites in Erbil. The study evaluated the effectiveness of environmentally friendly nZVI synthesized from lemon peel extract in removing chromium, nickel, and cadmium from contaminated soils.

## Methodology

Soil samples were collected from areas near an oil refinery in Erbil and analyzed for heavy metal contamination. Green nZVI nanoparticles were synthesized using lemon peel extract as a reducing agent. Remediation experiments were conducted to assess the efficiency of heavy metal removal.

## Key Findings

The study revealed significant contamination and remediation results:

- **Soil Contamination**: Elevated levels of chromium (52.85–79.54 mg·kg⁻¹), nickel (41.10–63.42 mg·kg⁻¹), and cadmium (1.41–1.76 mg·kg⁻¹) exceeding baseline standards
- **Remediation Efficiency**: Green nZVI achieved 12% removal of chromium, 5% removal of nickel, and 6% removal of cadmium
- **Sustainable Approach**: Lemon peel-synthesized nZVI offers an environmentally friendly remediation method
- **Practical Applications**: Demonstrates potential for field-scale soil remediation

## Implications

These findings have important applications for:

- Remediation of industrial contamination sites
- Development of sustainable nanotechnology solutions for environmental cleanup
- Protection of soil and water resources in industrial areas

## Conclusion

The study demonstrates the potential of green-synthesized nZVI nanoparticles for heavy metal remediation, providing a sustainable approach to addressing soil contamination near industrial facilities in the Kurdistan region.
