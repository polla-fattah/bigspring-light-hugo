## Abstract

This comprehensive genomic study sequenced SARS‑CoV‑2 Delta variant samples collected from West Erbil Emergency Hospital in the Kurdistan region of Iraq. The research identified the variant as belonging to lineage B.1.617.2 and characterized multiple spike protein mutations, including several unique substitutions not previously documented in Delta isolates.

## Methodology

Whole genome sequencing was performed on SARS‑CoV‑2 samples from confirmed Delta variant cases. Advanced bioinformatics tools were employed to analyze mutations, identify lineage classification, and compare findings with global genomic databases.

## Key Findings

The genomic analysis revealed significant insights:

- **Lineage Classification**: Confirmed as B.1.617.2 Delta variant lineage
- **Spike Protein Mutations**: Identified 13 distinct mutations on the spike protein
- **Unique Substitutions**: Discovered four novel mutations (E156G, T250I, T19A, and L861W) not previously reported in Delta isolates
- **Regional Characteristics**: The Kurdistan region samples showed specific mutational patterns that may have implications for transmission and pathogenicity

## Implications

These findings highlight the importance of regional genomic surveillance in tracking SARS‑CoV‑2 evolution. The identification of unique mutations underscores the need for:

- Continued monitoring of viral evolution in the region
- Assessment of vaccine efficacy against region-specific variants
- Understanding potential impacts on diagnostic and therapeutic approaches

## Conclusion

This study contributes valuable genomic data to the global understanding of SARS‑CoV‑2 Delta variant diversity, emphasizing the critical role of regional surveillance in pandemic response and preparedness.
