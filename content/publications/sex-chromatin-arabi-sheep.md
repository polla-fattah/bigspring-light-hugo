## Abstract

This longitudinal study examined 122 Arabi sheep in Erbil over a 14-month period (July 2014 to August 2015) to assess the relationship between sex chromatin morphology and productive performance, particularly milk production and reproductive traits.

## Methodology

The research monitored 122 Arabi sheep throughout the study period, documenting sex chromatin shapes and measuring productive performance indicators including daily milk production and various reproductive parameters. Statistical analysis was conducted to evaluate repeatability and heritability estimates.

## Key Findings

The study revealed significant findings regarding sex chromatin and performance:

- **Milk Production**: Sex chromatin shapes showed significant effects on daily milk production patterns
- **Reproductive Traits**: Chromatin morphology influenced multiple reproductive performance indicators
- **Repeatability**: The repeatability coefficient for daily milk production was estimated at 0.42
- **Temporal Patterns**: The longitudinal design allowed for assessment of consistency in performance across seasons

## Implications

These results have practical applications for:

- Breeding programs in Arabi sheep populations
- Predicting productive performance based on chromatin characteristics
- Understanding genetic factors influencing milk production and fertility

## Conclusion

The study demonstrates the significant role of sex chromatin morphology in determining productive and reproductive performance in Arabi sheep, with practical implications for livestock breeding and management strategies in the Kurdistan region.
