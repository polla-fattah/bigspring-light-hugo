## Abstract

This comprehensive study examined 132 local black goats to investigate the relationship between sex chromatin morphology and productive performance traits. The research analyzed the influence of different sex chromatin shapes on milk production, fertility rates, and birth weight characteristics in this indigenous goat breed.

## Methodology

The study evaluated 132 black goats, documenting their sex chromatin shapes and measuring various performance indicators including daily milk yield, fertility rates, and birth weight. Statistical analysis was performed to determine correlations between chromatin morphology and productive traits.

## Key Findings

The research revealed significant associations between sex chromatin shapes and productive performance:

- **Chromatin Shapes Studied**: Drum stick, sessile nodule, tear drop, and small club morphologies
- **Milk Production**: Goats with drum‑stick chromatin produced the highest daily milk yield (≈681 g day⁻¹)
- **Fertility Impact**: Sex chromatin shape significantly affected fertility rates
- **Birth Weight**: Chromatin morphology was associated with variations in birth weight
- **Trait Relationships**: Strong correlations were observed between chromatin shape and multiple productive traits

## Implications

These findings provide valuable insights for:

- Selective breeding programs targeting specific productive traits
- Understanding genetic markers associated with performance in local goat breeds
- Improving livestock productivity through chromatin-based selection criteria

## Conclusion

The study demonstrates the important role of sex chromatin morphology as a potential marker for productive performance in local black goats, offering practical applications for livestock improvement and breeding strategies.
